\section{Cross-Lingual Transfer and Steering}

\subsection{Cross-lingual transfer}

Probe transfer establishes that the toxic-to-detox signal is not uniformly
language-invariant. Recovered cross-language steering artifacts include
language-specific directions at encoder layer 6 and English-to-Yoruba/isiXhosa output
comparisons. In the archived copy-rate table, English-transfer steering produces zero
exact copies for both Yoruba and isiXhosa, while the within-language copy rate is
0.0333 for Yoruba and 0.0000 for isiXhosa.

These copy-rate values show that transfer directions alter generated behavior. They do
not establish an improvement in non-toxicity, meaning preservation, or acceptability.
The final controlled study therefore compares paired no-intervention, within-language,
English-transfer, matched-norm random-direction, and shuffled-pair-direction outputs
using frozen test examples.

\subsection{Exploratory Amharic strength sweep}

A recovered mT5 sweep applies an encoder-layer-6 steering direction to the same 50
Amharic examples at strengths 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, and 5.0. All strengths produce
non-empty outputs, and all 50 outputs are unique at every strength. chrF
reference overlap peaks at strength 3.0 (0.4292), compared with 0.3916 at strength
0.5. At strength 5.0, reference overlap falls to 0.4154 while similarity to the toxic
input rises. This is consistent with a non-monotonic strength--utility trade-off.

The sweep remains exploratory because the archived direction is test-derived, there is
no strength-zero baseline, there is no independent non-toxicity outcome, and the result
is not replicated across seeds.

\subsection{Controlled steering confirmation}

\textbf{Result slot for final rerun.}
The final intervention analysis will estimate directions from training data only and
select layer/strength on development data only. It will evaluate paired outputs under
no intervention, within-language direction, English-transfer direction, matched-norm
random direction, and shuffled-pair direction. We will report paired bootstrap
confidence intervals for human non-toxicity, meaning preservation, fluency,
acceptability, semantic similarity, reference overlap, copying, emptiness, length,
and output diversity.

\subsection{Human evaluation}

\textbf{Result slot for returned annotations.}
We prepared a multilingual sample of 500 outputs across nine languages and four model
families. The protocol assigns at least two annotators per output and collects binary
non-toxicity and acceptability judgments and 1--5 meaning-preservation and fluency
ratings. The final version will report completed annotations, agreement statistics,
per-language counts, and paired comparisons for the controlled steering conditions.
